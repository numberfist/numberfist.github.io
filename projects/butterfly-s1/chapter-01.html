Chapter 1: Vanisher

 

Lecture Hall 5 at MIT was busily filling up, despite the large lecture hall size. Students filed in in large clusters through the two entrances on either side of the lecture hall, as they glanced around the room towards the professor and then towards the lined up seats as they took to their positions from the lecture before.  Some looked lively, some looked half asleep already.  Some carried notebooks, backpacks, or nothing at all.  Ruth Cyn, a freshman like most of the others, stepped in quietly.  Her straight black hair fell slightly past her shoulders, and she was dressed simply in soft, warm tones, wearing a light to medium pink full sleeved shirt above a regular white T-shirt, and neat, yet unassuming jeans.  Pinned to the top of her shirt was a delicate 🌸 brooch with a softly colored light yellow interior surrounded by 5 equally spaced pale pink petals.  There was a softness to how she moved even as she climbed up the stairs, with her arms tucked close and her eyes darting around the room.  Her soft pink oversized backpack bulged behind her, dwarfing her figure.  Ruth settled into her seat, near the front third of the lecture hall, shrinking slightly into her seat before opening her notebook on the foldable desk in front of her and placing a rainbow of gel pens in careful order.  Her notebook pages were already meticulously inked and color coded in neat, miniscule, dense handwriting.  Mira, a confident girl, also a freshman, took her seat next to Ruth, one seat closer to the aisle.  She wore a dark blue unzipped jacket on top of a green shirt, along with a pair of jeans, a brown belt along her waist and a watch on her left wrist.  Her brown ponytail hung over her right shoulder.  She looked at Ruth and waved kindly.  Ruth nodded timidly without speaking.  Professor Lansen walked to the centerline of the room and stood in front of the projector screen, his posture tall, yet relaxed.  The moment he waved his hands, the projector screen dropped and the room instantly quietened.

“OK class, I hope you all have had a nice day so far… it’s only morning so of course you have!”  He paused briefly to allow the quiet in the room and the vibe to settle.  “Today we’ll be discussing the basics of atomic structure.  We now know that atoms are not the smallest piece of matter, however, in the 1890s it was still commonly believed that atoms were indivisible pieces of matter.”  He clicked his remote, and the slide changed to show a diagram of J.J. Thomson’s experiment.  “However, in 1897, J.J. Thomson performed an experiment that challenged this view.  On the screen is a schematic of the experiment.”  He clicked his laser pointer and circled the left side of the diagram. “The sample compartment contained H2 gas, which, when Thomson applied a high voltage to it, emitted a beam of cathode rays, which he passed through a pair of two parallel plates.”  He clicked his remote again and a dotted line representing the beam appeared on the screen.  “When he applied a potential difference across the two plates, he observed that the beam was deflected.” He clicked his remote again, and the plates were labeled with + and -, with a new beam deflected towards the + plate.  “However,” Professor Lansen continued, “it turns out that if you increase the potential difference even further, another beam appears.”  He clicked his remote to show a second deflected beam, deflected slightly towards the negative plate.  “Now, can anyone explain, preferably in depth, what’s going on here?”

A few hands went up, while a tentative silence filled the room.  Ruth slowly raised her hand halfway up, while Mira raised her hand confidently.

Professor Lansen pointed to someone a couple rows behind Ruth.  “Yes, and your name, please?”

“Elliot,” came the reply.  Mira frowned and turned around to look at Elliot, while Ruth flinched slightly as she lowered her hand.  Mira’s gaze lingered slightly on Ruth, her attention caught by the flinch.  She quickly turned her attention back to Elliot.

“The plates apply a uniform electric field,” he explained confidently.  “Since the beams are charged, they get deflected by the field, and the deflected beams hit the detector at a certain distance from the center.  This distance is directly proportional to e/m, where e is the charge of the particle in the beam and m is the mass.  Hence, the beam attracted towards the + plate consists of negatively charged particles, and has a larger e/m than the positively charged beam attracted towards the - plate.  Assuming the beams have similar charges, the negatively charged particles have a lower mass than the positively charged particles.  As a start, the negatively charged beam consists of electrons.”

“Well… that’s excellent, Elliot,” Professor Lansen responded.  He gave a nod of affirmation.

Mira lowered her hand slowly, slightly clenching her jaw as she turned back to face the professor.

“After that, this next part shouldn’t be too difficult,” Professor Lansen continued.  “Can anyone guess which beam consists of which?  Elliot already mentioned the electron beam, but what about the other?”

Mira’s hand went up again.  Ruth’s hand went tentatively up, before retreating again, shrinking just a little more into her seat.  Professor Lansen pointed at her.  “Yes?”

Ruth flinched slightly as she coiled back, almost imperceptibly.

“Don’t blame you,” the boy said.  “Anyone who saw literal superheroes fight an invisible supervillain is bound to be shaken up.”  
“Yeah, you could say that,” Nishant replied.
“I’m Aiden by the way,” the boy said.
“I’m Nishant,” Nishant replied.  “Nice to meet you.”
“Hey, aren’t we both staying in Apley Court?” Aiden asked.
“Yes, I guess so,” Nishant replied.
“We should meet up more often,” Aiden said.
“Sure,” Nishant replied, nodding.  “I’d like that.”
