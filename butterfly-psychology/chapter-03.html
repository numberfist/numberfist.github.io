Chapter 3: Sociovestigator

Source-text advisory: This chapter references Jhumpa Lahiri’s “Interpreter of Maladies” in a classroom setting. This chapter itself stays within the intended TV-PG age range of this story, and does not require knowledge of Lahiri’s original text. Since Lahiri’s text in its entirety may not be suitable for younger readers, it is not retold or discussed in full here. Readers do not need to look it up to follow the chapter. This advisory is included for readers who may be considering reading Lahiri’s original text separately.

Classroom 4-253 for MIT 21L.003 lecture was a compact seminar room. Eighteen or so students sat in a loose circle, with Professor Cormier seated among them near the far wall.

“OK so let’s continue our discussion of ‘Interpreter of Maladies’.  What stuck with you?”

Lily raised her hand.  Professor Cormier nodded.  “OK this might sound weird, but I liked how nothing dramatic really happens besides two people talking, and somehow that was a big part of the story.”

James leaned back slightly.  “Yeah, it’s less of arguing, and more like they were hoping the other person could read them without them having to say it out loud.”

Professor Cormier nodded.  “Nice, you’re noticing how Lahiri builds emotion with absence.  What’s not said can sometimes carry more weight than what is said.”

Grace raised her hand.  Professor Cormier nodded.  Grace straightened.  “Mr. Kapasi wants his job to make him feel seen, and Mrs. Das wants his job to mean more than it can.”  She paused, as the room went quiet for a moment.  “Look at Mr. Kapasi’s interpreter job.  It reflects what he wants to be in his own life, and what he wishes someone else could be for him, someone who understands.  You would think that being an interpreter would bridge that gap, but ironically, he ends up as someone who can listen, but can’t respond in a way that feels meaningful to either of them.”

A brief silence filled the room.  Professor Cormier smiled and nodded thoughtfully at Grace.  “Wow, that was… a really good way to describe it,” Lily finally said.  Grace smiled lightly and warmly.

“That’s what I liked about the story, but the ending felt a bit off,” James added.  “It was like it just… stopped.  But also it didn’t?  It’s a bit hard to put my finger on it.”

“I think that’s the point,” Grace responded, looking at James.  “It looks like a resolution, but it doesn’t feel like one.”

Professor Cormier nodded.  “Yes, that’s a very strong way to put it.  Everyone, Grace brought up a strong point here.  Writing something that looks like resolution unless you pay attention is a hard thing to write well.”

Pencils and pens scribbled.  A few students glanced at Grace and nodded thoughtfully.

“Are there any other impressions any of you got while reading?” Professor Cormier asked. Ruth shifted in her seat.  “Yes, Ruth?” Professor Cormier asked.

Several heads turned to Ruth.  Ruth lifted her gaze slowly and hesitantly. “Um… I was just thinking… maybe there’s something about how Mr. Kapasi… doesn’t explain himself,” she ventured quietly.  “Like… even when he talks to people… he still feels closed off.”  She paused.  A few students leaned in slightly.  Ruth glanced down and fidgeted with the corner of her page.  “So he’s… sort of unreachable at times… and when he thought he finally had something… it just slipped away.” Ruth continued, without looking up.

There was another pause.  Lily smiled.  “That’s actually really interesting,” she said brightly, her voice breaking the silence.

Ruth looked up quickly, and gave a quick shrug.

Professor Cormier nodded slightly.  “That’s a good read, Ruth.  The story keeps reminding us that silence is not the same thing as absence.”

Lily looked at Ruth reassuringly.  Ruth’s face flushed a light pink as she tapped her pencil lightly and quickly against the page of her notebook, and her gaze remained fixed on her notebook.

“OK, hopefully this can get you started on the essay prompts,” Professor Cormier said, scanning the room.  “We’ll continue our discussion next lecture, so please have some thoughts prepared.”

Students shuffled as they packed their stuff, zipped their backpacks and began filing out of the class.  Grace stood up and swung her backpack over one shoulder.  Emma stepped towards her.  “Grace, your comment about irony earlier really stuck with me. It’s like the story is so much more clear now.”
Grace smiled warmly.  “Thanks, I kept thinking about your point about the setting, and how they all seemed close together, but still felt emotionally distant and lonely.”
“I agree,” James said to the two of them.  He looked at Grace.  “You’ve got this elegant way of tying things together.”
They exchanged brief smiles.  A little closer to the back, Ruth quietly zipped her backpack and brushed her hair behind her ear without looking up.  Lily stood up beside her.

“I liked what you said in class today,” Lily said to Ruth quietly.  
Ruth shifted slightly, and her shoulders drew inward and her eyes lowered.  “Oh…” Ruth’s voice trailed off.  “That just… came out.”  Her hands clasped at the straps of her oversized backpack.
“Still, it was a really good point,” Lily said reassuringly, giving Ruth a small smile.  “I hope you talk more often.”
Professor Cormier addressed the class.  “I’ll be in my office if anyone wants to discuss the readings, and I’m also willing to check your drafts if you want.”
“Thank you Professor!” Grace said cheerfully to Professor Cormier as she turned towards the door.
“You heading to office hours?” James asked Grace as they walked out of the classroom.
“Probably in a bit, after I scribble down some ideas before I forget.”
“Bet they’ll be just as sharp as always,” James chuckled.
They headed out.

In the back, Lily turned to Ruth. “You coming to office hours?”
Ruth nodded quietly.  “Yeah…” she said hesitantly.  “Just in a minute.”
Lily smiled.  “Cool.  I’ll come.”
They both exited the now empty classroom together.

The interior of Professor Cormier’s office in Building 14 was peaceful and cozy.  The walls were lined with books, and Professor Cormier sat in her swivel chair in front of her wooden desk.  On the other side of the desk, Emma, James and Sophie were already seated in the semicircular arrangement of chairs, 5 of which were empty.  The door opened with a soft click, and Lily and Ruth stepped in quietly.  Ruth kept her posture tight, as if trying not to take up space, and she lowered her gaze as she surveyed the room.  Lily offered a quick smile to the group as they found seats near one of the walls, with Ruth sitting in the far right of the arrangement of chairs, and Lily sitting next to her near Kayla who sat two seats away from Lily.  Kayla and Lily exchanged a friendly wave.

“Glad you could join us,” Professor Cormier said to the two of them.  “Any thoughts or questions on your minds?”

There was a pause, and Ruth lifted her hand hesitantly.  Emma, James and Sophie glanced her way.  Professor Cormier nodded.  “Um… when Mr. Kapasi started translating… it’s like he felt… at least kinda there, like… like he just wanted to disappear into the job… just to belong there?”  She looked down at her lap as she finished, and fidgeted slightly with her sleeve.
Professor Cormier nodded.  “So you’re saying the job gives him a presence he doesn’t feel he has elsewhere?”  Ruth nodded slightly.
“That’s a good reading,” Professor Cormier commented.
Lily glanced at Ruth.  “See? You’re always so insightful.”  Ruth shrank slightly and looked away as her face warmed.  “Oh… It just… came to mind.” she said hesitantly, and so quietly that Lily could barely hear it.  Ruth tucked her hands into her sleeves, and drew her shoulders more inward.  She turned her gaze slowly towards Lily, and then towards Professor Cormier.
“Um… if it’s OK I had one more thing?” Ruth asked hesitantly.  Professor Cormier nodded.  “Um… about Mrs. Das, I was also thinking that maybe even while she was opening up, she was still afraid to stay open,” Ruth continued quietly.  “Like—”  Ruth faltered mid-sentence as the door swung open and Grace entered.  Ruth’s eyes widened slightly as she glanced at Grace, and her body turned sharply away from the door.
Grace scanned the room as she stepped in.  “So sorry!  I hope I didn’t interrupt something?”
Ruth flinched, and her breath caught. She turned sharply inward, her shoulders curling away from the door and into her body, her eyes wide and downward-looking.
“I– I’m sorry,” Ruth said faintly.
She rose fast, leaving her backpack on the floor, and shuffled out the door without lifting her gaze, her body fully turned away from Grace.
Lily stood immediately.  “I’ll be right back,” she told Professor Cormier quietly.  Professor Cormier nodded.  Lily quickly followed Ruth out.
A brief, awkward silence filled the room.  Sophie adjusted her sleeve.
Grace looked around, unsure.  “…Did I walk into something?” she asked hesitantly.
“Not at all,” Professor Cormier said gently.  “Welcome.  We were just discussing how Mr. Kapasi sees his own role in the story.  I’m hoping you brought your usual strong insight.”
“Thank you,” Grace said, relieved.  She casually moved towards the open seat near the door next to Emma.

Ruth stood alone in the hallway outside Professor Cormier’s office facing away from the office door, her body near one of the hallway walls.  Her arms were loosely folded around her body and her posture was small.  
Lily stepped out slowly.  “Hey… you okay?” she ventured.
Ruth didn’t move or meet Lily’s eyes.  Her gaze remained fixed on the floor.
“You didn’t have to leave,” Lily continued. “Everyone was listening.  I was listening.”
Ruth remained silent and motionless.  Lily shifted slightly, half-turning towards Ruth.
“That thing you said, about opening up… that stayed with me,” Lily said, more softly this time.  Ruth’s head dipped a fraction lower. “You didn’t have to say much,” Lily continued.  “And even still, you were… you were incredible in there.  I think… you don’t realize how much people listen when you speak.”
Ruth’s face flushed a light pink as she inhaled quickly and shakily.  “I– I’m sorry.  I didn’t mean—”
Lily moved closer to Ruth. “It’s OK,” she said gently.  “You don’t have to explain.”  Lily’s eyes remained on Ruth as Ruth glanced quickly through the open door.
“Do you want to go back in?” Lily asked comfortingly, while motioning to the open door beside them.  

Ruth silently shuffled one step forward toward the door, and peered around the corner and into the classroom.  Her eyes fell on Grace, who was engaged in an excited conversation with Professor Cormier, who in turn nodded brightly and enthusiastically.  Ruth inhaled sharply as she took two abrupt steps back, recoiling from the door.  She lowered her head silently.
Lily glanced toward the open door, where Grace’s voice carried faintly, then back to Ruth.  “It’s her… isn’t it?” Lily asked quietly.  Ruth stood still and motionless without replying or meeting Lily’s eyes.  “You know, you don’t have to be loud to be brilliant. You…” her voice trailed off. “You stay with people.”
Ruth’s face warmed as her arms tightened slightly around her body.
“You matter in there,” Lily continued, filling the silence.  “More than…” She hesitated.  “More than some of the louder ones.”  She paused, looking more intently at Ruth.  “Don’t worry, it’s OK,” she said reassuringly.  “I’ll go in with you.”
Lily began walking towards the door, and after a few steps, looked back at Ruth, who stood hesitant and motionless.  Lily smiled at her warmly.  Ruth followed Lily, her head down and arms wrapped somewhat tightly around her body.

Lily re-entered Professor Cormier’s office, closely followed by Ruth.
Grace turned towards them and smiled warmly.  “Hey, Ruth?  Are you okay?”
Ruth met her eyes for the briefest moment, then lowered her gaze and quickly scurried to her seat, her body slightly turned away from her.  Lily sat beside Ruth, positioning herself between Ruth and Grace.

“I’d like to return to your thought from earlier, Grace,” Professor Cormier said.  Grace turned to look at her.  “I thought it was quite nuanced,” Professor Cormier continued.  “If you don’t mind, could you elaborate on that?”
“Sure,” Grace said cheerfully.  “So I was thinking about the ending again.  Mr. Kapasi is really looking forward to a connection, and to him it feels important, but the story seems to dismiss it almost casually.  We don’t even get to see how Mr. Kapasi felt afterwards.  I think that’s why the ending felt so unresolved.”
“That’s an excellent observation,” Professor Cormier replied, nodding thoughtfully.  “The story lets loss happen almost in passing.”
Ruth shifted slightly, opened her mouth, but stopped.  Lily glanced at Ruth.
“Ruth?” she asked encouragingly.
“Maybe it’s because… it was close enough to hurt?” Ruth ventured quietly.
“That’s a delicate point,” Professor Cormier responded, nodding.
Ruth folded into herself a little more.  She glanced at Professor Cormier in the front, then at Grace to her left, and then looked quickly at her lap.
“See?  You always see the things no one says.  That matters,” Lily said softly to Ruth.  Ruth’s eyes remained downcast as her arms wrapped around her body.
Kayla scooted one chair closer, so that she was sitting next to Lily, and leaned over Lily towards Ruth.  “Yeah, Lily’s right,” she added reassuringly.  “You don’t say much, but when you do, it’s really nice.”
Ruth gave a slight shake of her head as she wrapped her arms tighter around her body.  Her face warmed as she shrunk further into herself.
Lily smiled at Ruth, patting her shoulder gently.  “See? Told you.”

The class is back in group discussion with Professor Cormier who sits at her usual spot near the back of the room. Desks are arranged in a loose circle. Grace sits near Sophie, and Emma. Ruth is on the far side of the room, slightly turned away, and Lily and Kayla sit on either side of her, with James sitting nearby.

“OK so we’ve heard some good thoughts today,” Professor Cormier said, addressing the class.  “So let’s continue with Mr. Kapasi.  From your perspective, what else defines him?”
Emma raised her hand thoughtfully.  Professor Cormier nodded.  “I kinda got the impression that he wasn’t fully present in either job.”
Professor Cormier nodded.  “Interesting point.”  She scanned the room for more raised hands.  “Yes, Grace?”
“I think the tour guide job matters just as much.  He can describe things clearly from the outside, but his role keeps him there.”
Professor Cormier sat up.  “Nicely put.  There’s notable tension in that limitation.  Please continue.”
“With the interpreter job it’s the same thing.  He can describe things precisely for other people, but still can’t be as involved as he wants.”
A thoughtful pause filled the room.  Professor Cormier, Emma and Sophie nodded.
“I—” Ruth said softly, barely audible.  The class turned to her.  Ruth hesitated as her eyes darted between Grace and the rest of the class, and her body shrank slightly.
Lily smiled at Ruth.  “Ruth?  You were going to say something.”
“Please, Ruth, I’ve been wondering what you’d say,” Kayla said to Ruth, softly and encouragingly.
Ruth spoke faintly.  “I think… maybe… he was afraid of being hurt again…?” Ruth’s voice trailed off.  Lily, Kayla and a few students near her leaned in.  Ruth glanced at Grace, and quickly looked away as she took a short, slightly shaky breath.  Ruth tightened her arms around her body and turned her shoulders away from Grace.
There was a slight pause.  Lily beamed at Ruth. “See?  That.  That’s it!” she whispered to Ruth.  “I’ve been trying to say something like that, but you got it!”
James, sitting next to Kayla, nodded casually at them. “I know, right?  It’s like you feel what we can’t,” he said in a low voice to Ruth.
Professor Cormier nodded.  “That’s a good way to understand his distance.”
Ruth’s body and gaze turned downwards and away from Grace, as she squeezed her arms tighter around her chest, and her cheeks warmed to a faint pink.  
Kayla looked briefly at Grace, before turning to Ruth.  “Some people just talk a lot trying to sound smart.  They’re not worth your attention.”
Ruth remained motionless, her gaze fixed downward at her lap.
Professor Cormier scanned the room.  “OK, I think that’s a wrap for today’s class.  Please start looking at the essay prompts early, so that you can leave yourselves some time to think about excerpts to choose.”
Students began stretching, packing up, and standing up.  As Professor Cormier stepped out of the room, Lily leaned in towards Ruth, as James and Sophie approached her.  “Hey… you alright?” Lily asked tenderly.
Ruth shifted slightly away from Lily and Grace’s side of the room.
“You know, I think you might be better at this than any of us… and that’s OK,” Lily said comfortingly.  Ruth glanced up quickly at Lily before looking away.  Her face remained tight.
“Seriously Ruth, Lily’s right,” Kayla said, leaning towards Ruth.  “Honestly, what you say sticks with me… even only when you say a little.”
Grace slung her bag over her shoulder and glanced at Ruth with a gentle smile.  Ruth’s eyes widened as her eyes met Grace’s, and she flinched suddenly, as her body turned abruptly away from her.
Lily sat down next to Ruth.  “You don’t have to look at her, Ruth.  Don’t let people like that shut you down.”
“It’s OK to be the best, you know,”  James added.  “You’re allowed to be yourself.”
Ruth’s shoulders tightened as she curled away from Grace.  Grace’s smile disappeared, as her eyes flicked towards Ruth one more time, before she turned and left.  After a few seconds, Ruth uncurled just a little, her shoulders loosened and her chin lifted by a fraction of an inch.
Lily’s voice dropped.  “It’s okay,” she said comfortingly to Ruth.  “She’s gone now.”
Ruth glanced quickly at the door, then quickly looked downwards towards her lap.
“You know… you don’t have to shrink like that, Ruth,” Kayla said warmly.
“Yeah, we’re here for you.” James added.  “All of us.”
“I– I’m sorry, I–” Ruth stammered as her breath caught.
Kayla shook her head quickly and firmly.  “Hey, no, it’s OK.  Don’t ever apologize for being who you are.”
“Exactly,” James confirmed.  “Don’t worry about it.”
Ruth fidgeted with her sleeve.
Sophie took a small step towards Ruth.  “You didn’t do anything wrong,” she said softly to Ruth.
Ruth’s fingers remained clenched around her sleeve.
“It’s not your fault, Ruth.” Lily said quietly and firmly. She inched closer to Ruth. “Some people can’t stand it when someone’s better than them. That’s their problem, not yours.”
Ruth’s shoulders folded inward and her head dipped.  As her face blushed, her hands slipped from her sleeve and covered her cheeks, and she remained silent and wordless.

Grace smiled back.  “Deal.”

Grace looked at Emma, then Professor Cormier.  “My feeling was that he wanted to be noticed by someone who didn’t already expect something from him.  So he turned every detail into meaning, because for him, that’s all he had left.”
Professor Cormier smiled and nodded.  “Nicely put, Grace.  You’re pointing to how much even small signs of connection mean to him.”
Across the semicircle, Ruth’s breath caught.  She opened her mouth, then closed it quickly without speaking and looked down.  Her eyes briefly flicked to Grace, then back to her notebook as she fidgeted with it.

Grace sat alone on the bed, curled up without her blankets.  Her phone screen showed 10 unread texts from Nadia, technically read but not unlocked.  The fox plush lay partially tucked under the pillow some distance away from her.  Footsteps approached the door.  Grace looked up, and Nadia stood in the doorway in her pajamas, holding tea.

Grace took a single look at Nadia.  The silence filled the room.

“Grace, I’m sure—” Nadia started.
“You weren’t there!” Grace snapped back as she sat up abruptly.  Nadia blinked.
“You didn’t hear it!” Grace continued.  “You didn’t hear the way they said it!”  She made air quotes with her fingers.  “Worse than… someone else!  Jealous of… someone else!  Like I was just someone they all hated!”
Grace stood up and looked Nadia directly in the eye.
“And worst of all,” Grace continued, “I have no idea what I even did.  I must have hurt… someone.  I must have said something wrong.  That’s the only explanation.”
“Grace—” Nadia tried again.
Grace walked closer to her.  She lowered her voice, but it was bitter.  “Do you know what it feels like to be accused of something you don’t remember doing?  How it feels to be punished for something you can’t even name?”

“Whatever I did, it must have been bad.  That’s the only thing that makes sense.”
“I… don’t think that’s fair to yourself,” Nadia said gently.
“IT’S NOT ABOUT WHAT’S FAIR!”  Grace spun around fiercely to face Nadia again.  Nadia recoiled slightly.  Grace paused, then clenched her fists by her side.
“I think I need some space,” she said finally, quieter now.  “Please.”
Nadia nodded, then left silently.  Grace crashed down on her bed, with her legs dangling over the side, and carefully massaged the fox plush between its ears.

“Well maybe she doesn’t need Scarletbug,” Tikki said gently.  Nadia blinked.  “Maybe she needs you, Nadia!” Tikki finished.
Nadia blinked again.  “You’re right.  Well, here goes nothing.”
Tikki flew into her pocket as Nadia opened the door to Grace’s classroom.  Grace sat alone at her desk.  She hadn’t moved since the battle, and stared at the floor, motionless.  The moment Nadia’s eyes landed on Grace, she rushed up to her and hugged her tightly.  “It’s OK,” she said quickly.  “It will all be OK.”
Grace didn’t hug back.  “No,” she said slowly and quietly.  “No it won’t.”  She paused.  “No matter what I do, I always end up doing something wrong.  I don’t even mean to.  I… I was even rude to you, of all people!”
Nadia pulled back slightly, holding her by the shoulders.  “It’s OK.  It happens.  You were just overwhelmed.  I’ve said things I didn’t mean too, so many times, and that doesn’t make me a bad person, does it?”  She smiled gently.  “If you want to overthink, then we overthink… together?”  Grace remained silent and motionless.

At least seven of the students rushed towards the bathroom around the corner in the hallway, huddled together.  The sound of soft whimpers and sobs seeped from the propped-open bathroom door into the hallway.  Emma delicately set Ruth’s soft pink oversized backpack on the ground beside her.
“Can you believe how absolutely vile that Grace is?” Lily sneered through grit teeth, her voice low and full of disgust.  “Making someone like Ruth, someone so sweet, so perfect, so defenseless, shake uncontrollably in a stall for FIFTEEN WHOLE MINUTES?!”
“She was shaking the whole time, Lily,” Kayla said, half horrified.  “Actually shaking.  You can hear her even now!”
“And Grace just had to attack her like that,” Sophie added.  “Who is she to just demand Ruth speak up?  Ruth’s only like this because of her!”
“It’s not the first time either,” James continued.  “I see it now.  Grace has been hurting her on purpose this entire time.  Her true colors were going to come out sooner or later.”
“She wanted to break Ruth,” Zayden added.  “And she actually did.”
The group nodded silently.  The sound of sobs and whimpers stopped, and a tense stillness set over the hall.  Ruth emerged slowly, her arms wrapped tightly around her chest, her face flushed, and her eyes wide and glassy, trembling and breathing shakily.  Her eyes instantly grew wider at the sight of the group in front of her.  Lily rushed forward and squeezed Ruth in a tight embrace, as the others watched with worried expressions.
“Ruth!  It’s OK!  We’re here.  We’ll protect you,” Lily said between gasps.
“You didn’t do anything wrong, Ruth.  You never did,” Kayla said reassuringly, stepping closer to Ruth.  The group moved with her.  “You were just being you.  Sweet, brilliant, kind.”
“We saw it.  All of us,” James said comfortingly.
Ruth’s buried her head in Lily’s shoulder, collapsing completely into Lily’s arms as muffled choked hiccupping sounds escaped.
“I— I– I’m so s-sorry I e-exist—” she said delicately between the sound of whimpers and choked squeaks.
“No, no, no, you matter,” said Lily, as she hugged Ruth tighter.  “Please don’t say that?  Please? It’s not your fault that Grace couldn’t handle being worse than you at everything.”
Emma let out a deep breath and embraced Ruth tenderly from the other side.  “Ruth, I’m so sorry,” she said quietly.  “I should never have trusted her.  Please forgive me?  Please?”
“She doesn’t deserve your kindness, Ruth,” Kayla said firmly.  “Not someone as jealous and hateful as her.”
“If she tries to hurt you again—” Zayden started.
“No.  We’ll make sure she never gets the chance to set foot near you!” James finished.  “She won’t even get to try.”
The group closed in gently and protectively as Ruth continued to tremble and squeak softly into Lily’s shoulder with shallow, shaky breaths.

Argus, fully clad in his hooded black cloak, walked quietly through the hallway.  His eyes scanned the classrooms as he passed them, but almost mechanically and automatically.
“Must have missed a small detail, my poopybutt,” Argus murmured to himself, quoting himself.  “That convinced them somehow.”  He paused.  “But not me.  I’m definitely missing a much bigger detail.”  He stopped at one of the classrooms.  The lights were on, but the classroom was quiet.  Argus pushed the door slightly open, and saw a girl sitting motionless and slumped in her seat.  “Excuse me,” Argus asked politely.  “Was there a supervillain here recently?”
Grace turned to look at the mysterious figure that had stepped inside the classroom.  Her face was flushed and worn tired, and her eyes were red, wet and puffy.  “What’s it to you?” she asked bitterly.
“I’m just curious.  Please tell me, if you don’t mind.  Was there a supervillain here recently?”
“This too?” Grace said sharply.  “Why do I have to tell you anything?  I don’t even know you!”
“Then I think you’ve already told me what I needed to know,” Argus said, walking up to her and stopping just short of her personal space.  “But certainly, that’s not all.  I can’t help but feel your frustration.  Do you really think hiding it will do any good?  Wouldn’t you rather want… a clean way out?”
“From what?” Grace snapped.
“From whatever just happened,” replied Argus, pacing around her.
“You don’t know anything!” Grace replied back.
“Exactly.  That’s why I’m asking.”  He paused.  “Something… or rather, someone, happened.  So what was it?”  Grace didn’t respond.  “You don’t have to tell me everything,” Argus said, more softly this time.  “We both want to understand this better.  How did it begin?”  Grace shifted uncomfortably and looked away. “Sometimes, it’s enough to just think through what happened.” Argus said reassuringly.
“I don’t know.  It was like— I walked into the room, and suddenly, I was wrong, out of place… She just looked scared, curled up, trembling, didn’t even say a single word, never hurt anyone.  But everyone looked at me like I’d done something unforgiveable,”
“And did you?” Argus asked.
“No… at least I can’t remember.  I did my best to be nice, to give her space.  But everyone rushed to comfort her, like I was some threat who kept hurting her.”
“Did they say anything?” Argus asked.
“They said I was jealous of her, that I was worse than her at everything, that I wanted to hurt her!”
“And who is her?  Who are they?”
“What’s that to you?” Grace asked defensively.
“It’s nothing to me,” Argus replied matter-of-factly.  “But it’s everything to you.  Wouldn’t it be easier to keep track?  You do want to get to the bottom of this, right?”
“Fine,” Grace said grudgingly. “Lily. Kayla. Sophie. James. God, I can’t even keep track of all of them. They all swarmed me while … Ruth, Ruth Cyn…” She inhaled sharply. “Wait a second, who even ARE you?” Grace shot up out of her seat. “Why am I even telling you this?”
“Because you needed someone to tell,” Argus replied. “But that’s not the important part. The important part is what you do next.” He lowered voice to a much softer tone. “So, you’re frustrated, angry, and afraid it’s going to spill out when you can’t control it. Don’t deny it. I can feel your anger towards every single person you named, and it’s not going anywhere. So instead of ignoring it… what if you could use it for power?”  Grace looked at him quizzically. “Enough power to stop the ones who hurt you?” Argus continued.
Grace stiffened. “What good would that do?” she objected. “Look what happened to me just by dealing with it!”
“At this rate, there will come a time where your anger will be too much to control. What are you going to do then, wait for someone to twist you again? You want to vent, right?” Argus asked calmly.
“What do you think I want to do?” Grace retorted. Argus stepped closer to her. “Then vent.”
“Really?” Grace said, half-sarcastically, half in disbelief. “Last time I tried that, I was attacked!”
“Your anger was used against YOU,” Argus continued calmly.  “Why not use it against THEM?”  He paused.  “They want to call you worse than this… Ruth person at everything?  They want to twist every reaction you have against you?  So, what if you instead used your anger to become better than Ruth at everything AND all of them combined?”
“That’s impossible!” Grace objected.
“With enough anger, it’s totally possible. And fortunately for you, you already have exactly that,” Argus replied. “Tell me, what would they do then, when they can’t call you jealous of Ruth, worse than Ruth, or whatever else they were calling you?” He paused. “They’d be completely at your mercy, instead of the other way round. And after what they’ve done to you, you get to decide how much mercy they deserve.”
“I refuse to hurt anyone!” Grace objected. “Enough of —”
“I never said anything about hurting anyone,” Argus replied, unfazed. “In fact, you should never hurt anyone. That way, whoever started this will spiral, knowing that they failed to destroy you and can’t do anything about it. They can’t lie their way back, they can’t call you out for something false, they can no longer hurt anyone, especially you, they can only DRIVE THEMSELVES INSANE in an endless loop of their own malice as long as they keep trying. They got to watch as you were broken. Is this not proper justice?” He paused again. “What do you say? Use your anger, and finish this, or let this continue day after day after day? The choice is yours, and unlike them, I’ll respect it.”
Grace remained silent. Her expression was neutral.
