Chapter 12: Conjugator

 

Another Harvard-focused episode.  This is more of a cooldown episode for the institutional plot (after Yurina’s exposure, while also opening the Spring semester).  It is the start of the semester, (such as the first week).  Aiden, due to his Knightclaw responsibilities being unpredictable, decides to take slightly easier classes than he could take otherwise.  Luciano, seeing this, mocks/gaslights him on it but Aiden clocks it instantly and leaves the situation, but when he privately speaks with Plagg shortly after, it’s revealed that it still hurt.  Meanwhile, Luciano mocks Yurina regarding the events of Chapter 11 saying things like “look at you, you can’t keep up so this is what you do? No wonder you run to the TAs and even your own peers begging them to solve the homework for you.” or “STS winner and you still need everyone else to do your work for you?” and since Yurina gets swept into the conflict anyway, this might be a good place to seed Regeneron’s response to Chapter 11, such as Regeneron contacting Yurina or maybe Yurina impulsively volunteers that they found nothing (the real reason that she knows being that she didn’t falsify or plagiarize enough in any visible way.
 Author note for what’s actually going on here: What she did do is use her usual tricks to flatter the mentor into doing the project for her, then passed it off as her own work while conveniently leaving the mentor out in the competition using the same bubbly enthusiastic flattery she usually does. So a surface level plagiarism audit won't catch her, but this Regeneron STS backstory is still more than sufficient to explain how she is so good at cheating so blatantly and recklessly while getting away with it. On the Math 55A take home final, she used the same trick on her classmates, and just like Regeneron, it worked on them, and would have gotten away with it completely if not for Decoupler’s akumatization).  But unlike Aiden, Yurina is easily baited by Luciano, and Luciano takes advantage of that to keep poking at her.

The main plot of the episode begins or the above can be interweaved with it: Inigo is planning on taking Math 129, and so is Luciano.  Luciano sees Inigo asking questions and keeping up, and meanwhile Luciano also finds that he’s struggling to keep up, thinking he could handle Math 129 and was better than Nishant the idiot who only is taking Math 123 (this is Luciano’s subjective interpretation), hoping to mock him later in reference to his gaslight of him in Chapter 4, not knowing that Nishant is taking Math 123 to prioritize fundamentals to learn the material more deeply and systematically instead of jumping ahead for clout.  Finding that he might end up dropping Math 129 instead, leaving him with no algebra class to take should that happen (because he’d have to play catch up in the other class), Luciano decides to gaslight Inigo into thinking that he’s too stupid to handle Math 129.  For the first lecture of the semester, Inigo thinks he’s got this.  For the second lecture, Luciano realizes he can’t keep up enough and gaslights Inigo into thinking he’s too stupid to handle Math 129.  Inigo then later mentions that he’s planning on dropping Math 129 and skips the third lecture.  Inigo, after the lecture, later gets independent evidence from a classmate or someone that his questions were appreciated and extremely helpful (maybe even questions no one ever thought of asking), or that an explanation he thought was dumb (thanks to Luciano) was actually quite clever, or that the lecture he missed clarified something he had started understanding but gaslit himself (thanks to Luciano) into thinking he really was too stupid at all.  Inigo confronts Luciano, but Luciano simply argues that if Inigo was that willing to drop then he was never cut out for it in the first place, and insists that it’s entirely Inigo’s fault, and that he was just looking out for Inigo before he embarrassed himself with his stupidity.

Meanwhile, Lepidon needs a cooldown akuma in a familiar environment (waiting it out isn’t enough, Lepidon needs to interrupt the pattern of akumatizations itself, and thus needs a cooldown akuma to do it), so he akumatizes Inigo knowing that since he didn’t get involved with the situation as Nishant, even if the heroes start monitoring anyone with Math 55 ties again, he can honestly say as Nishant he didn’t know anything about it.  With Inigo akumatized, Inigo’s akuma power as Conjugator is to force anyone he chooses to obey their own judgement, be it Luciano, a TA (good foreshadow of the incompetent TA akuma that will become a strike on science fair culture), or even the heroes.  If Luciano says you should drop, and Inigo forces him to follow his own advice then Luciano ends up unenrolled from Math 129. If Jared or Harry agrees with Luciano then they get hit too, etc.  If Knightclaw tells Scarletbug to retreat, and Conjugator hits Knightclaw, then Knightclaw gets kicked from the battle (this also shows Lepidon adapting since Polariton, he’s slowly starting to find other ways to disrupt the heroes’ verbal coordination without needing Polariton and an intense direct attack to do it).  This means that Conjugator can force someone to follow the judgments or advice they have expressed to others, even when those judgments were hypocritical or insincere.  Then the akumatization finishes, and Lepidon slowly pieces together the pattern that Ruth, classes she manipulates, Mrs. Spencer, Luciano, an undergrad TA, etc. all have some variant of arrogance from incompetence, it’s just the manifestation that differs.  So now Lepidon has at least some model to know about what to expect when incompetence combines with arrogance, be it an improper rejection from a position, dropping, gaslighting, a coordinated attack by someone like Ruth, etc. even if he doesn’t formalize it as such due to lacking the formal knowledge of terminology to do so.  Over the process of the akumatization he realizes that “follow your own advice” is actually an effective non-magical counter against Luciano.  But to be sure, he just needs to test that himself.  So he de-transforms back into Nishant and leaves his dorm for Annenberg.

The heroes, once again suspicious about Math 55 and the fact Nishant was a target there during S1, decide to check on him.  Nishant uses the alibi of not being connected to the Luciano or Inigo incident, but now that they mention it, is willing to check back on Inigo after hearing their description of the akumatization.  Nishant later reassures Inigo, subtly turning him against Luciano.  Inigo asks how Nishant knows and Nishant “admits” that he wasn’t alone either, and that his friend Aiden helped him (even if Aiden sees this, he just sees Nishant being helpful just like he was to Nishant and thus isn’t suspicious that it’s part of manipulation).  Inigo silently realizes that he wasn’t actually alone, and that Lepidon was indeed with him.  Thus Nishant uses a credible Nishant-compatible way to get Inigo thinking Lepidon is helpful, allowing him to keep Inigo around  to push back against any “but the holder is an akumatizer so they’re clearly evil” not because Lepidon cares about how he’s perceived but because it will make later institutional resistance from ACS/Regeneron/etc. far more difficult for them.  The best part for him is that Inigo will do it of his own accord, and completely honestly from his POV.

Possible post-credits scene: Nishant finds Luciano, and finishes him with the “ask a question I dare you” since by then, he’s learned over the course of the akumatization exactly how to hit Luciano where it hurts.  Since it’s still 8 chapters later, it’s NOT Nishant immediately knowing what to do, he’s had time to think AND an akumatization to nudge him there.  But Nishant could go even further since he learns from Inigo during the akumatization that people found Inigo’s questions clever, meaning Nishant can push harder when Luciano resists: “please don’t tell me you’re too stupid to even know what question to ask?”

Alternatively, the “possible post-credits scene” is part of the main chapter and this is the post-credits scene instead, or replaces the “possible post-credits scene” as the post-credits scene: Luciano's clique starts to destabilize due to some accidentally applying Luciano’s “advice” to themselves thinking they’re not good enough to remain near Luciano, some realizing that being in his clique doesn’t actually protect them socially, and leaving or other factors, and meanwhile Luciano ends up dropping Math 129 while Inigo ultimately stays.  But Inigo still has to deal with the consequences, he still needs to catch up on the missed lecture, and his relationship with the clique isn’t restored either, he might hold distrust or resentment towards them for “betraying him” even if they leave the clique for the reasons discussed above.  Meanwhile, Inigo silently remembers Lepidon as helpful, allowing Lepidon himself to keep Inigo around.
