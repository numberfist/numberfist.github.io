Chapter 10: Secret Knight

Ruth’s snap of Chapter 9 leaves behind a fractured aftermath.  People aren’t very sure how to deal with that, some feel betrayed, others feel a social pressure to stay with whatever the safer social thing to do is, even if that means “switching sides”.  Some just disengage out of feeling guilty, some were in it for a sense of belonging?  And some don’t really know.  Meanwhile, Ruth no longer stuffs her backpack, because it (and her vulnerable act in general) is fried, so from her POV there’s no point in wasting effort in an act that’s stopped working.  She’s better off spreading rumors at this point, because what else can she do, even if it won’t work?  Meanwhile, Ruth’s targets like Grace, Elliot, Hazel, and whoever the physics target is, are somewhat vindicated socially in the sense that people talk to them and acknowledge them, even if nobody apologizes.  Except one case: Emma tries to apologize to Grace.  Grace isn’t sure if she wants to accept it.  When it came down to it, according to Grace, Emma made her choice.  Grace refuses to just pretend that didn’t happen.  Emma leaves her alone for now, hoping that maybe things will cool down.

But meanwhile, word of Ruth’s snap inviting scrutiny on her begs the question: “If things went this wrong with trusting Ruth, who can we trust?  What can we trust?”  That’s when bystanders of Chapter 9 realize: USNCO campers (more generally, US*O campers) can be trusted (in general, campers can be trusted).  I*Oers can be trusted.  After all, when Ruth was faltering and her friends were faltering, who stepped in?  The campers, and the I*Oers.  What starts as trusting people who demonstrated competence during Ruth’s collapse quickly becomes a hierarchy in which competition credentials determine whose opinions deserve to be taken more seriously than others on the fly.   But this quickly leads to a status competition, who has better status, who is therefore more competent, and this is where people like Quentin and Yasmine (IChO golds of the most recent cycle) and Anirudh, Alan, Avinash (IChO golds of the cycle 1 year before) and campers like Norman or Nileton (this is a placeholder name for now) come in.  However, Kevin, a USNCO Top 50 who didn’t make Top 20, starts feeling left out despite feeling that he could easily stand toe to toe with these guys, after all, who made countless study notes?  He did.  Who managed CODS?  He did.  But in the heat of Ruth’s collapse and the struggle for “then who can we trust” the campers and I*Oers become insensitive and in some respect, contemptuous and rude.  When he tries to enter, he’s ignored (Alan doesn’t even look or speak to him, and that itself, amidst the status fight that is emerging, might even be enough without an insult at all).  Or a small insult pushes Kevin all the way to akuma ready (it’s a disproportionate reaction and that’s the point, it’s not isolated, and there’s a history behind it even if the reader won’t know that at the time): they get to ride on camper or I*Oer status and leave him out just because of that.  It’s not like they don’t have secrets of their own.  Worries of measuring up.  Something! So why do they get to be so high and mighty?  Even if he does prove himself it won’t matter anyways.  He’s still the guy who didn’t make camp.

This was Lepidon’s plan when in Chapter 9, he suggested Null Hypothesis consult people with competition experience: to set this into motion, knowing that someone less aggressive, contemptuous or scornful than the rest would get akuma-ready first, especially someone who is less willing to cut corners with status (and someone less willing to cut corners even during USNCO, but that cost him Top 20 when they used his notes to study and now get to dismiss him as they please).  He doesn’t know that Kevin will be akuma-ready first, he just knows that someone like him will.  He doesn’t even need that someone to be completely honest or possess clean academic integrity, he knows (from his own experiences that won’t be mentioned by the chapter to keep it a mystery for future chapters) that such a person with competition experience at MIT is unlikely to exist, so manually searching or counting on the existence of such a person isn’t a good idea.  But whoever comes closest (even if it isn’t very close) might be close enough to have both the motive and ability to carry out what Lepidon intends for Secret Knight: a first strike on the competition culture using the status power struggle caused by Ruth’s leaving a power vacuum due to her snap, and the fact that MIT has a lot of competition veterans, with some more honest about it than others, and can thus act without needing micromanagement or excessive convincing during the pitch: they already feel the status war get to them, have a motive to act in the direction of secret exposure, and don’t need to be told where the problem is.  He doesn’t know who will get akuma-ready first, only that someone coming close enough (even if that close enough isn’t very good) will, and, due to what he’s steered Ruth’s snap into setting into motion, that person will get akuma-ready faster than the others.  Then just akumatize whoever that happens to be.  So the plan is robust, but it relies on Chapter 9’s fallout behaving as he expects, hence why he needed to set that into motion during Null Hypothesis’ akumatization itself (which happened when Null Hypothesis asked for chemistry competition veterans and Norman, Alan, Avinash came in).

Lepidon akumatizes him into Secret Knight, whose power is as follows: he can choose the depth of the secret, and then extract it from a person he chooses.  The secret appears in his chainmail book.  He can choose to forget the secret and not reveal it, keep the secret and not reveal it, or keep the secret and reveal it to others.  For example, someone who claims to be tricked by Ruth might have found a sense of belonging and a chance to be socially superior in the group while it was there.  Quentin is found to consider “I still can’t calculate/explain my [IChO] score.” but there is a reason behind it: “I didn’t get my test back, so how could I know?  Maybe I just surprised myself, and I really am that good.”  However, Secret Knight doesn’t find it immediately.  When he looks for deeper secrets, he might find something he chooses not to repeat.  For someone else, he might find “I still sleep with a teddy bear” as more secretive or embarrassing to a person than “I inflated my contribution to my ISEF project and somewhat downplayed my mentor’s contribution to possibly concerning extents.”  A similar secret (possibly to a greater extent) could be exposed for STS.  For Chapters 7 and 9, Ruth’s absence of competition experience becomes a point that people are surprised to learn about, but in Chapter 10, it’s revealed that… she actually did!  She qualified for ISEF, but used her fragility act in her local fair to do it.  Some decide “this isn’t representative of ISEF, this is just Ruth” others think that “The fact that this slid at her regional fair is concerning” and now people aren’t sure if ISEF people can be trusted.  But of course that’s somewhat jumping to conclusions, so what actually happens is that ISEF becomes more precarious, and Ruth’s reputation takes another hit.  But this still spells impending trouble for people like Nadia, who also did ISEF in high school.  Same idea for Quentin, there’s a split in how his secret is interpreted by others, but it skews heavily to Quentin’s justification, because campers/IChOers did get a chance to prove themselves in Chapter 9 and they did… but Ruth was ISEF and didn’t, then there’s the other ISEF competitor who was secret pulled by Secret Knight…

Secret Knight’s power also threatens the superheroes in battle, since technically he could reveal or gain access to their secret identities if they are hit, but that doesn’t happen: Scarletbug and Knightclaw think that this is a result of successfully evading shots from Secret Knight, in reality, Lepidon wasn’t interested in secret identities.  But either way the threat or hypothetical threat creates pressure for the superheroes, and Lepidon accepts that without going out of his way to push it via Secret Knight.

So Lepidon comes out of the akumatization knowing where the pressure points are, and having made what he considers (based on prior backstory knowledge) suspicious exposures, but in practice it gets swept under the rug in some way or another.  Not completely though.

But coming out of it, Kevin’s akumatization doesn’t go unnoticed.  After all, he’s extremely influential on CODS due to his prior activity and involvement and position, even campers who are mods on CODS aren’t as influential on the server.  So that carries weight even if people aren’t sure what to make of it.
