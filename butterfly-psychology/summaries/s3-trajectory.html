Season Trajectory: Season 3

 

The S2 finale left a huge question of credibility among Regeneron, US*O and even I*O.  With it, the people in charge and institutions in charge, like Society for Science, ACS USNCO, APS USAPhO, etc. have come under scrutiny from the public and the people who once drew their credibility and prestige from them are now facing suspicion of dishonesty and cheating.  Even Nishant, because of his IChO credential.  Of course, Nishant accepts this as a temporary cost: now that everyone is under scrutiny, people who can’t substantiate their credentials will be exposed as unable to do so, for those who can, there’s no problem: they just show what they’re capable of, honestly.  And this is exactly what is happening. The whole time, Lepidon spent S2 in the shadows.  But now S3 opens with the scrutiny being “let the IChOers, campers, whoever, back themselves up.”  People demand open access and transparency, so opacity becomes less and less a viable option for institutions like ACS USNCO: too many people are demanding it.  It’s not like other Olympiads are as opaque.   So problem solved… right?  Not really.  Dishonest and/or dysfunctional institutions (that aren’t even dishonest at all) won’t go down without a fight, and unlike some of the competitors associated with them, they’re not afraid to play as dirtily as it takes to save their reputation even at the expense of participants who still played by the rules.

Even still, the broader question remains.  So dishonesty (ACS USNCO especially regarding camp selection and camp+), bad procedure (Regeneron, vulnerable to insufficient procedure, and this was explored throughout S2, but it’s not corrupt), have been revealed and exposed, enough that the general public can see it.  But what’s stopping them from overgeneralizing?  MIT gets hit from the pattern of admitting as many competition people as they do, it’s a huge portion of the admitted class.  So what?  MIT, the MIT, can be fooled by a credential?  After all, it’s not like S2’s akumas took place somewhere else, no, they took place at MIT?  So all it takes to get in is to just fake your way through ISEF, slap it on a resume, and you’re in, just like that?  S2 made it a viable point of skepticism.  That does not mean every admit made it in through dishonesty (Nadia certainly didn’t).  Same logic for Harvard, it had Yurina, the Chapter 15 akuma, the incompetent TA incident, etc.  Does that mean everyone at Harvard cheated their way through?  No (Nishant is a counterexample, and so are several others).  And with MIT and Harvard under doubt, what stops universities and university admissions in general from all falling under doubt?  Is it simply that EVERYONE cheated?  A large scale crisis in academia is imminent. And it’s not like the universities have a magic formula for this situation.  MIT really has been using prestige labels as a "get into MIT free" card. Take that away, and MIT has no idea how to do its next cycle. What's it going to do, look at each application more carefully? They've never really tried it before in that particular way, so that's still error prone because they're new to whatever it is they'll have to do next. Then they have to handle public reputation. Student newsletters can't be trusted to align with institutional PR.

And even still, the question still stands: what next?  Can the exposed institutions find a way to function properly, or will the vicious cycle just continue?  What exactly goes behind a power structure, and authority?  Too little authority gives indiscriminate chaos.  Too much authority leaves room for lack of accountability and abuse of power and procedure.  Something in between might be anything from the solution, or something totally useless.  The current system, as revealed by S2, is clearly not working.  So what does?  This is a much harder question than S2’s question.  Hence the premise of S3.

S3 Premiere: Fallout and Confrontation

The fallout of the ISEF/STS exposure results in Regeneron scrambling in a similar way as in the IRL scandal.  

Meanwhile, ACS USNCO finds itself under pressure to re-check the campers and IChOers of the most recent cycle and the cycle before it, and so on.  Even the IChO itself starts coming under doubt: how were they even vulnerable to something like this in the first place?  How did a single country (US) manage to get this far?  And who’s to say that other countries aren’t doing the same behind closed doors?  People are demanding that the original IChO tests be released.  They can be checked against the answer key independently.  IChO refuses in the name of privacy of its participants, and says “getting the tests for each country is a country-specific matter.  As the host country we must be impartial and cannot intervene.  If you have questions, you are free to approach the relevant participating country directly.”  Given that ACS USNCO was also coming under suspicion due to the S2 finale, public scrutiny turned there.

But Dr. Rodney withholds them, saying that “it is illegal and against the law” for her to release student tests to the public.  In reality, what happened is that when the S2 finale happened, Rodney was quick to wipe the records.  Why such a bold move?  Because it was never said in writing in the official rules that she couldn’t do that, nor was it said in writing that she could.  So technically, Rodney never broke the rules.  She simply exploited that loophole.  But as she keeps insisting on the “illegal and against the law” explanation, someone brings up “where’s the rule?”  Another brings up “Fine, you won’t release the tests.  But we can’t trust the scores anymore?  So what can we trust?”   Rodney says “Fine.  We’ll make a test similar to the IChO.  The contestants will be assessed on the same test on the same rubric.  If it takes something like THAT to convince you that we were honest this whole time, fine.  Let’s see how people do.”  But Rodney could easily just pull the same tricks as last time.  What if the mentors (in this case, the coordinators specifically hired for this) aren’t impartial?  What if there’s a trick like last time?  Even if there is no trick, Chapter 14’s akumatization already revealed why simply re-administering the IChO with different numbers won’t prove anything, the people being tested could just have studied the past IChOs as preparation, and they would pretty much know the whole exam they’ll be given.  Then EVERYBODY does well, and ACS uses the result to vindicate the people who were bumped on the real IChO.  Or ACS could have a trick up their sleeve that they only use behind closed doors, it’s not that they haven’t done that before. There is no way to tell… and Lepidon knows this.  So he comes up with a plan.  He’s already am evil supervillain in the eyes of the public, and in the eyes of Rodney, the one who put ACS in this mess by means of its own actions.  So he can do something that Nishant can’t do, not even an akumatized villain can do, but only Lepidon can do: scare her so much of facing accountability without being able to fudge her way out of it, that she actually doesn’t dare to try anything funny.  To do that, he needs to pay her a visit in person.  And just in case, he’s prepared for Scarletbug to be the one enforcing it.

So he akumatizes himself to give himself the power to teleport to DC, then rejects and re-akumatizes himself into Polariton so that he can slip into ACS HQ unseen.  Then he hides, rejects the akuma, and transforms into Lepidon.  And now he can approach Rodney personally.  So he does.

But wait, why does Lepidon just expose himself like that, isn't that reckless?  If the heroes get called in, they would find him, face to face.  They would learn his profile now, and he now risks that they could just yoink the Miraculous off his chest.  What's he going to do, fight them?  They're the better combatants, even if he’s trained hard to hold out in a fight (during and after Polartion, with the Polariton duel literally being preparation for a moment like this: should he have to come face to face in a fight with the superheroes, he’s not helpless).  The reason: until now showing up in person didn't have a strategic benefit.  Until now.  Nothing is stronger for Dr. Rodney than seeing the very supervillain that brought down her USNCO and put her an inch within resigning, standing in front of her saying "Fine.  Do a fair retest like you said you would.  But try anything funny and I will know." and Rodney, once she realizes who the person in front of her is, will KNOW that this is no empty threat.  Lepidon doesn’t even need to be specific.  S2 already more than proved that he can deliver on his words.

So he approaches Rodney and requests her to “Please, forgive my intrusion, but I was hoping we could have a quick little chat.”  Rodney slowly realizes who she is speaking to.  But still isn’t willing to believe it.  So Lepidon makes it clear.  “I’m sorry.   I should have introduced myself.  You may call me Lepidon, holder of the Butterfly Miraculous, and the one behind each superpowered incident.”
Rodney replies: “But why confront me? You could have gone to Regeneron, right? Right?  Didn’t you expose them too?”
Lepidon: "Regeneron's failures were due to terrible procedure and insufficient checking of contestants.  They never deliberately lied through their teeth.  You did. I know you did." And now Rodney is stuck, because Lepidon isn't wrong. And she has no idea how he knows, what he knows, how much he knows, just like she has no idea how he dismantled ACS USNCO from the outside so thoroughly in the eyes of the public with nothing more than dark butterflies. So clearly he knows enough to follow through with the threat of "try anything funny and I will know."  So this is far more than just accepting Lepidon’s words at face-value, whoever this is, he clearly knew enough to put ACS USNCO into a crisis, and that is no minor threat, and she has no idea what more he knows anyway).

As a quick note: During S2, Lepidon really does learn that searching for corruption in Regeneron dead ends. The procedure and vetting is so bad it's easily exploitable.  An institution does not need to be corrupt to be exploitable.  Corruption requires deducing intent and actions that bad procedure alone can’t explain. He's seen the clues of bad procedure in Regeneron since Chapter 10, even if he only really understands the difference much later in S2.  What if Rodney goes scorched earth and tries to warn the other Olympiads and Regeneron? After all, Lepidon literally exposed himself to her by showing up physically as himself, the Butterfly Miraculous supervillain, the akumatizer. Lepidon knows she won't. How? Trendposter already taught him that: someone that badly cornered is more likely to take the others down with them, than nobly go down warning the others if they stand nothing to gain, and something to lose (they become the one who got exposed) by doing that.  So Rodney is running out of options.

So Lepidon continues and tells Dr. Rodney, "I agree.  Your idea for a retest was a really good idea, and I support you in that.  So write a test, if you must.  Not an identical one though.  But try anything funny, and I will know.  I will know." and look at what's happened.  The same Dr. Rodney, who lied to Nishant's face immediately after the IChO, is unwittingly, terrified of the person standing in front of he… who only the audience knows… is Nishant himself, and this time she doesn’t dare lie.  So she hurriedly calls Scarletbug, who shows up with Knightclaw.  And now Scarletbug and Lepidon confront each other.  Should it come to a fight?  Lepidon planned for that… after all, he had his reasons to be so interested in combat training from the heroes.  He'll need that here, or he's not surviving an encounter with two experienced superheroes in hand to hand.  So Nishant's contingency of "learn combat from the superheroes themselves should it come down to it when I have no 'hide away the akumatized object' and my chest brooch is in yoink range, I can at least not get insta shotted by a cataclysm wielder with exponentially better reflexes and a in the field planner who is better at short term battle tactics" and that's why he deliberately didn't use his akuma powers in the final fight of the Polariton duel.  In a situation like this, he won't have them anyways, and he’s prepared for that situation.

He says this full well knowing it's probably Nadia, and thus he already knows that Nadia seeing a new IChO-like assessment isn't cheating, she's not under IChO scrutiny anyways and there’s no conflict of interest.  Therefore Scarletbug accidentally seeing the new US IChO test won't break the integrity of the test itself.  The same cannot be said about Lepidon, who is a former IChOer.  Thus, Lepidon never planned to personally supervise Rodney anyways. And Scarletbug is perfect for the job, provided he can manipulate her into doing it for him.
But Nadia's POV is different, and not even Lepidon has the full POV.  "You brought down ISEF, and now people like me, Nadia, suffer for it!  You don't get to talk."  But she can't or she reveals to Lepidon that she did ISEF, and a superhero should never drop clues to their identity.  Superheroing 101.  She doesn't know Lepidon already knows her identity and thus knows her character and background, and that's why he's trusting her.  But supervising Rodney's administration of "the demonstration IChO" is within her ethical boundaries.  She'd rather be the one supervising than a literal supervillain.  And that's exactly what Lepidon wants Scarletbug and Rodney to think.

But how does Lepidon know that Scarletbug won't just cave the moment she is alone with Rodney?  That’s because he already knows of Scarletbug's honor and in S2, even stress tested it.  So by S3 he knows she's honest even under stress, having disproven his own former suspicion of Scarletbug's dishonesty during S2, and that as Nadia, she isn't affiliated with USNCO, so no conflict of interest arises if she supervises Rodney instead.  So the identity arc serves more than just "villain knows hero so he can manipulate her in the conventional sense of knowing where her buttons are and how to push them", this becomes how Lepidon can make a gamble like leaving Rodney under Scarletbug's supervision and know that the gamble will pay off, that there's no conflict of interest or risk that Scarletbug's morals will fail from Rodney pressing her, he tested Scarletbug's conviction in S2 already to know that, and knows she's Nadia and thus not USNCO affiliated in any way.  Lepidon only need maximize that threat he poses to Rodney (and he knows Rodney won't cave unless she's terrified within an inch of her position) and Rodney just needs to understand who he is to know that this is no empty threat.

But once that cools down, that still has consequences:

  1. Now there's a possible arc for S3, and that is the Guardian arc. S2's question was "when powerful institutions engage in misconduct and have the resources to cover it up, is there anything that can actually be done?" S2's answer is "Yes, if a determined enough adversary has the methods and access to do so." S3's question is "So then what? Is there really a sufficiently robust zero-trust system that can be trusted to remain operational, or is this just too good to be true? What's going to keep holding extremely powerful systems accountable from here on out?" People are MUCH more willing to ask these questions, but that's useless without a solution. Now look at the hero side of things. Absolutely, the heroes will tell the Guardian they personally fought the butterfly holder. And guess who's in charge of extremely powerful systems of superpowers? The Guardian. And nobody is supervising him. This becomes a much more salient question that can be easily folded into the themes of S3. After all, it would continue a pattern S2 already had. Once the institutional and superhero plots are entangled, they won't just magically unentangle just because S2 is over.
  2. Regarding the retest, Rodney still has a loophole that beats “change the numbers of the original IChO so that everyone knows how to do it.”  And that connects Dr. Reed, who is really important for the USNCO backstory, with Dr. Rodney, Quentin, S2's introduction of Reed, and this S3 fallout. The better Lepidon’s akumatization in Chapter 14 succeeds, the bigger loophole Dr. Rodney gets here. In Chapter 14, Lepidon gave Quentin something that did more than change the numbers on problems he's already seen: it actually tested him conceptually, even if he just studied the pieces of the IChO he got wrong, after the fact. Now look at what Rodney is trying to do in S3: Same thing. Reed already called Quentin and got his account of the questions he was given, in Chapter 14 (S2E6). Once Reed has the questions, roughly, all it takes is for Reed to give what he knows to Rodney, and now it becomes the same problem over again. Rodney sets the test based on that, and now Quentin gets the advantage anyway, and they succeed in protecting their actions regarding Qentin during the actual IChO.  So ironically, the better the demonstration Lepidon does in Chapter 14, the bigger the advantage Quentin gets. But Nishant also knows what he's looking at once the test comes since he was still Lepidon in Chapter 14 and knows what was set. So the result: Quentin and Nishant get distinguished from the rest… and now Quentin looks partially vindicated. But… surely Lepidon saw this coming? Wouldn't he adjust Chapter 14 accordingly? No, Lepidon couldn't have seen this coming at all. Lepidon had every reason in Chapter 14 to suspect that exposing Quentin would be all that's needed, when that fails to expose him as thoroughly as he thought, that he needs to retrace his approach. He has no reason to suspect that this will come back to bite him after his exposure already succeeds, and every reason to suspect that the exposure itself, for the most part, was endgame since the exposure is already a huge achievement for Lepidon.  So he wouldn’t see it coming unless he omnisciently predicts Rodney would fight back with a retest, but how would he predict that? He instead predicts Rodney to either succumb to questioning and get exposed, or invent a thinly veiled lie just like he's already seen her do in the illegal and against the law lie, and this time get caught. So, in practice the result would actually be: Nishant crushes the “retest” because he's seen it and worked through it mid-akuma in Chapter 14 (so he can do it under duress), Quentin crushes it despite not crushing it in Chapter 14, the rest are thrown into the deep end. So the result: Quentin really did outperform, on the “retest”, exactly who he was expected to, except Nishant. Quentin won't speak because he has no incentive to and knows better. Nishant can't speak either, because he can't know something only Lepidon could. And clearly Quentin wouldn't just tell Nishant: Quentin would never help a competitor, so Nishant can't even use Quentin as a cover story. The same safeguard (Quentin can't collaborate with others on his grilling) that served Chapter 14, seals Nishant's mouth in the S3 premiere.  ACS USNCO gets a chance to shove the Chapter 14 incident under the rug, while vindicating Quentin, and to this both Nishant and Rodney have counters against the other.
