Chapter 13: Viscosia

 

Brittany (a former USAPhO camper now MIT freshman) is interested in Professor Feldman’s research on nonlinear fluid dynamics of oceans and planetary atmospheres (will revise this later).  So Brittany asks her friend Nadia for advice, knowing that she did a research competition, and Nadia tells Brittany just to be herself.  Max gets a slot as an undergrad in Feldman’s group (via resume inflation and dishonesty, but of course it’s not checked correctly), and now there’s only one slot left.  Ruth learns of this and now is intent on getting the slot, lest a former admirer/supporter (like Max) surpass her, and Ruth’s ego of course cannot tolerate that.  So Ruth attends Feldman’s talk, and so does Brittany.  Nadia comes with Brittany to support her, since Brittany seems a bit nervous.  Brittany, who is genuinely interested, asks a question during the talk about the fluid dynamics/nonlinear dynamics side.  Feldman answers the question and seems impressed, and meanwhile Brittany treats this as a good sign (Nadia, similarly, treats it as a good sign too).  Meanwhile, Ruth isn’t able to come up with anything to ask or comment on, and struggles to follow the talk, so she just sits there quietly with a small posture.  Meanwhile, Brittany, feeling encouraged, talks to a grad student or postdoc about joining the group, and leaves a decent impression on them: she’s technically competent, seems genuinely interested, a little more physics leaning than biology leaning but still a good fit.  So now Brittany is actively optimistic about getting a slot in Feldman’s group.  Ruth, meanwhile, sees this and hates it.  Later, “Brittany” (who is offscreen Senti-Brittany created by Ruth, as an author’s note that is not revealed to the viewers, what’s happened is that Ruth created a sentibeing between Chapters 7 and 8 to hold the Peacock Miraculous for her, and kept its amok in order to control them.  So she keeps the Peacock Miraculous off her person while retaining the ability to create sentibeings like Senti-Brittany) shows up to the grad student/postdoc and is suddenly dismissive and rude about the research, saying she only wants this for her resume and treats it all as too easy for her anyways, or that except for the nonlinear dynamics research everything else is trash or something along those lines.  The grad student/postdoc is confused but nevertheless rolls with it.  A few minutes after, Ruth shows up to the same grad student/postdoc before they leave, and presents as humble, interdisciplinary, eager, and low-friction (while also inflating her own resume A LOT, even lying). Not old full-force fragility, but enough self-effacing “I know I have a lot to learn” and “fragile humility” energy to contrast against “Brittany.”  Later, Feldman asks his student how it went, and the grad student/postdoc reports what he heard and saw, something like “Brittany is sharp, but I’m not sure she respects the whole project. Ruth kinda does, and seems more aligned with what we need, and seems easier to mentor.”  Now this is the point at which Feldman should know something is not adding up, this doesn’t sound like the same Brittany he met during his talk.  But Feldman is swamped with meetings, deadlines, projects, papers, grants, and whatnot.  So he takes the grad student’s assessment at face value, despite his earlier favorable impression of Brittany, and decides on Ruth without following up.  His student accepts his decision.

Meanwhile, Brittany wonders why she hadn’t heard back from Feldman, and Sanjana overhears or finds out, and the moment she learns that Ruth was going for the slot too, immediately warns her about Ruth.  Brittany thinks Sanjana is just exaggerating or being paranoid, and treats it as such while being polite to Sanjana.  Feeling confident, Brittany decides to check in with Feldman, confident that it’s a misunderstanding, or Feldman was just busy, only to find out that she didn’t get it and she didn’t seem like a good fit for the group (or some generic explanation like that), and insists that the decision is final.  The very next thing she sees is Feldman welcoming a self-effacing and polite Ruth to discuss projects, and politely dismissing Brittany saying that he has a meeting.  Now Brittany is confused and angry: “what the heck went wrong?  Max faked part of his resume and got in.  Ruth was barely competent if not incompetent and got in.  I was competent, enthusiastic and genuine and left a good impression, and didn’t!”  And now Sanjana doesn’t seem so paranoid after all…

Potential outline for the conversation right before Ruth shows up to discuss projects with Feldman:
“I’m sorry, Brittany. We decided to move forward with other students for now.”
Brittany: “Was there something wrong with my preparation? I thought the talk went well.”
Feldman gives a vague answer of some sort: “You asked a good question, but based on the follow-up discussions, I’m not sure this is the right fit.”
Then Ruth enters before the conversation can continue and Feldman welcomes her in to discuss projects, politely shutting Brittany out due to needing to take this meeting, leaving her to fume in confusion with no way to know what happened or argue for herself.

Meanwhile Lepidon is wondering the same thing for a different reason.  He was sure that these types of results could only come from an incompetent judge.  But MIT professors are rarely, if at all, incompetent.  Yet the same type of outcome happened.  So what gives?  So he akumatizes Brittany to find out.  Brittany, akumatized as Viscosia, has the power to create conditional barriers and filters, just like how she was shut out.  Viscosia has control on the condition, and where and how the barriers apply physically.  But during the akumatization, Senti-Brittany shows up to keep undermining Brittany’s reputation through the sentibeing, and while she’s at it, watch the show, not realizing the akumatized villain is Brittany herself just unrecognizable on purpose (the pretense Lepidon gives her for that is “you don’t want to draw attention to yourself and risk coming off as resentful.  But knowing what happened might be more helpful.  If you’re qualified then that knowledge can vindicate you, right?”).  Lepidon and Viscosia see this, and the way “Brittany” is acting, and are confused.  First Lepidon suspects it’s an illusion (he knows to consider this since in Chapter 4, he was Hartree Fox).  So he has Viscosia use her power to force “Brittany” to come into physical contact with another object.  If she’s an illusion, she’d disappear.  But that doesn’t happen.  So “Brittany” isn’t an illusion.  So who/what is she?  Now Lepidon starts suspecting that Ruth can make clones of some sort, since suddenly that starts explaining a lot of observations he was previously unsure about: how did Ruth suddenly start showing knowledge inconsistencies, why is there a second Brittany walking around, then Ruth is the one getting the slot over Brittany right around the time the second Brittany showed up, and retroactively ties it into his Chapter 7 confusion.  After the akumatization, Lepidon realizes in his villain tag that the real problem was lack of sufficient checking, and incompetence was just one way for that to happen.  Now (in his head even if not onscreen since the story might preserve the outward ACS image without contaminating it with Nishant’s prior knowledge of ACS USNCO just yet) he can retroactively explain how competent and most likely impartial (as in he has no evidence that the IChO graders are NOT impartial like he does for the US mentors) IChO graders can mess up so much that their error affected medal results.  If they had no proper time to check, corrupt mentors (this also relies on prior knowledge and backstory Nishant has of ACS USNCO during which he found evidence for corruption over more charitable explanations during his own USNCO and IChO experience, but this is left unrevealed to the viewers at this point in the story) could easily game and distort the results far beyond what is even mathematically possible given the exam performance itself.

Meanwhile, Brittany decides not to try again for Feldman’s lab: if he’s able to make wrong decisions so easily she decides it’s not worth her time.  Also she silently to herself, dares Ruth to keep up, knowing that she probably can’t.  Ruth on the other hand, got the slot, but fragility won’t work anymore when it comes to research.  She’s bitten off more than she could chew, and now has to deal with that.  So her project ends up just stalling uselessly since she can’t actually do it.  As for Brittany and Nadia, Brittany is now suspicious of Nadia, since being herself clearly didn’t work, and so she starts questioning: if being herself didn’t work, but underhanded tricks like the ones Max and Ruth pulled did work, then what exactly worked for Nadia?  Is she hiding something, like dishonesty?  It’s not like Secret Knight didn’t expose dishonesty around ISEF, and to go even further, Ruth did game her regional fair…  So Brittany, who’s still feeling the hurt of being displaced from Feldman’s group, starts suspecting that there really might be something to the scrutiny around ISEF beyond just one chaotic akumatization incident, and starts treating Nadia as if she may be secretly dishonest (even though in truth, she isn’t).
