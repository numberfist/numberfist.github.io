Chapter 11: Decoupler

 

After a long stretch of MIT-focused episodes, Chapter 11 steps back to the Harvard side of the story, establishes the effect of the recent MIT events on the social culture and more importantly the rumor culture and what the grapevine has been passing around in the meantime.  Harvard, after all, didn’t just disappear from the story because the camera wasn’t on it.

Then the main plot is as follows: Yurina attempts to cheat on the 55A take-home with Sudhi and Jared, but Natalie, who is already getting tired of Yurina’s constant interruptions, intrusions on her conversations and boundaries, and ignoring her and others like they don’t exist when she’s not interested.  On top of that, Natalie, due to being a physics major, is close enough to get enough insults from 55ers for not being in Math 55.  So seeing Yurina, who she’s already irritated with, cheat on the Math 55A take-home final using the same excessive “excitement”, “enthusiasm” and bubbliness, to do it and get away with it right in front of her, that pushes her to akumatization.  As Decoupler, she has the power to tag someone (provided she has a direct shot at them) with a specific criterion, be it “anyone who tries to collaborate with tagged person on the 55A final” or “anyone who battles alongside Knightclaw”.  If the tagged person takes the action (such as a tagged Yurina continuing to try and collaborate with Jared or Sudhi, or a tagged Knightclaw trying to coordinate with Scarletbug), then a tether/coupling appears between them (so Yurina gets tethered to Sudhi and Jared, or Scarletbug gets tethered to Knightclaw) and Decoupler can only control the coupling nature and strength of existing tethers, she cannot spawn tethers without a tag and an action by the tagged person to spawn it for her.  This way, Yurina’s continuing to cheat and subsequent attempts to cover it up implicate her (since those actions lead to tethering, allowing Decoupler to force them together or apart), and Scarletbug ends up tethered with Knightclaw, forcing the heroes to turn a physical interference into an advantage (maybe with a Lucky Charm and a sharp Knightclaw).  But if Yurina doesn’t cheat once tagged, then nothing happens.  Which makes sense, since Decoupler is more interested in unambiguously catching Yurina in the act instead of running around saying she cheated sometime before, since catching her in the act is stronger.  The result after-akuma is Yurina’s usual cheerful cover for narcissism is more recognized as manipulation by some (inside Math 55A via, say, Luciano, and outside Math 55 via Natalie), and conveniently ignored by others.  Due to this being an akumatization, it doesn’t get taken up by the honor council, despite Yurina violating the honor code, and thus academic integrity, in a way the honor council would usually want to pay attention to: the rationale they use is that it would set a dangerous precedent to allow akumatizations to be considered as valid in investigating academic integrity violations.  

There are also other repercussions socially Harvard-side (BF also got 1st place STS and is a math major and current undergrad junior, and treats others with arrogance and condescension, and his younger brother AF, a freshman aiming to major in mechanical engineering, is friends with Nishant and Aiden.  AF might mention in passing that BF is on edge due to what happened in the recent supervillain attack, and then Nishant could find out that both BF and Yurina have 1st place STS history… THEN Nishant can start deducing backwards: now that he’s aware of her Regeneron STS status (her getting 1st place is how Lepidon puts two and two together with his Chapter 10 findings: what he considers shameless cheating/plagiarism was widespread enough in ISEF/STS that it was almost as if the competition strongly incentivized it, but even that had an explanation with “of course that’s what happens when students learn to present other people’s work, like their mentors’ or copied papers’ work, as their own.”  But now he’s forced to reconcile “Yurina cheats so blatantly and remorselessly, and is too good at getting away with it to have just learned it overnight… So where did she learn it?”  That naturally points at STS, which Chapter 10 already placed under reasonable suspicion.  Then even Ruth’s ISEF qualification makes sense, just like Yurina used “excessive cheerfulness, enthusiasm and bubbliness” Ruth could have used the fragility act and gamed the judges in the same way she already gamed Mrs. Spencer, entire classrooms, etc. into thinking her to be something she’s not.  So Lepidon suspects that it’s extremely possible that the judges themselves are too incompetent to call nonsense, and that’s a reasonable enough conclusion to seriously consider as the leading theory with what he has so far.  The second takeaway for Lepidon is that Chapters 10 and 11 consecutively hit Regeneron, and Yurina being STS 1st place.  If he starts investigating what he thinks is the logical next step based on his intuition thus far (incompetence of judges) that could alert Regeneron quicker than he’s ready for.  

The villain tag scene can be Lepidon wondering where and how Yurina learned to cheat like that and not get caught because clearly she didn’t learn it overnight, otherwise everybody would be able to do it, and he has a counterexample: he can’t pull it off himself even if he wanted because he know if he tried he’d get caught, hence why he doesn’t try it.  Then his interaction with AF becomes when he puts it together).  There are also social consequences MIT-side, since quite a few MIT students secretly envy the Harvard students, MIT is already highly concentrated with dishonest status toxicity (as implied by Chapter 10), and then the next thing they find out is that an STS first place at Harvard blatantly cheated on an exam and appears to have gotten away with it somehow.  That’s got to build some resentment, especially from those already primed to resent her for her STS and Harvard status: Wait, that Yurina?  You mean STS first place Yurina?  Yurina is at Harvard, has 1st place STS, all that for a cheater? Did she really cheat... or is this some weird hoax? She gets to cheat at Harvard, she gets the 1st place status, while we're here? If that's how easily she cheats, who's to say she didn't do this elsewhere too?" There's shock and resentment mixed in. Even though Lepidon isn't thinking from the POV of someone already resentful, he is reaching similar conclusions as forced by his own logic: he genuinely can't explain how Yurina would be so practiced at cheating if it didn't come from somewhere recent and STS is the most recent thing she did, and the Secret Knight akumatization already put research competitions in question.  An alternate post-credits scene could be: After the Decoupler incident, the Math 55 staff privately discuss Yurina's suspected violation of the take-home final’s rules (aka honor code violation). However, because the most conspicuous evidence arose during an akumatization, they decide that pursuing the matter could set a dangerous precedent for academic-integrity investigations. Professor Aubert and the TAs terminate the discussion without referring the case to the Honor Council.
