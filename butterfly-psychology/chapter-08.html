You can in fact give a category-theoretic interpretation instead of the geometric interpretation that Kimberley is describing. We have the classifying space functor B : Grp --> Top. We can extend this to a functor B: EGrp --> ETop, where EGrp is the category of group extensions (objects are group extensions 1 --> A --> E --> G --> 1 and morphisms are commutative diagrams of group extensions that preserve G) and ETop is the category of fibrations (objects are fibrations F --> E --> B, denoted p: E --> B, and morphisms are base-space respecting maps, that is, if p: E -->B and p': E' --> B are two fibrations over the same base space B, a morphism f:E --> E' must satisfy p(e) = p'(f(e)) ). Then B is fully faithful and thus gives an equivalence of categories between EGrp and the full subcategory of the image B(EGrp) in ETop. Furthermore, the fundamental group functor pi_1 is its quasi-inverse.

In the end it depends on your personal preference for a geometric formulation or an algebraic formulation.  Both interpretations are equivalent.
