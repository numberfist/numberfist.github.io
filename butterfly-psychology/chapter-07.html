Chapter 7: Registrar

Cold open: Professor Lansen’s office.

Professor Lansen walked to his ajar office door and pushed it open.  Ruth, with only her 🌸 brooch, followed.  Professor Lansen walked to his seat behind his desk.
“Thanks for meeting me on such short notice.”  He gestured to one of the seats in front of his desk.  “Please, sit.”
Ruth let her soft pink oversized backpack slip from her shoulder onto the ground and sat directly opposite to Professor Lansen.
“Knightclaw asked me to keep an eye on you after yesterday’s incident.  I just want to check if you’re OK.”  Ruth shifted in her seat and stared at Professor Lansen with a neutral expression.  “I’m available if there’s anything you need to talk about,” Professor Lansen ventured awkwardly.  Ruth remained still and silent.  A few awkward seconds of silence hung between them.  “Are you still comfortable taking the exam tomorrow?” Professor Lansen asked.  Ruth nodded faintly.  “I… I guess I’ll see you then,” Professor Lansen said.  Ruth quietly shouldered her oversized backpack, and left without a word.

Ruth raised her head slightly.  “It’s just ligands binding to a metal center, and they influence each other’s electronic structure and reactivity…” her voice trailed off.

Elliot opened his mouth to speak, but quickly closed it.

Ruth’s face further blushed and warmed as she lifted her face another fraction of an inch.

“I-it was just some diffusion equations for voltammetry,” Ruth replied hesitantly.  “I-I’m s-sorry.”

Ruth gave a small nod without meeting their eyes.  Sanjana shifted uncomfortably.
Elliot sank forward and buried his face in his arms on the desk.  From the back side of the lecture hall, blocked from Gary’s view, a dark purple akuma fluttered in and merged with the handout, turning it black.  Elliot abruptly sat halfway up as a telepathic voice spoke into his head.

“Hello,” Lepidon said into Elliot’s head.  “Forgive my intrusion, but —”
Elliot straightened and clutched his head.  “No.  Crystal Moth, Chrysanthia, or whatever you’re calling yourself, don’t bother!”
“My name is irrelevant,” Lepidon replied.  “But your situation isn’t.”
“You don’t know anything about my situation!” Elliot replied.  “Just don’t bother.”
“So that’s it?” Lepidon replied.  “You’ll just let them get away with it, just like that?”
“What else can I do?” Elliot retorted wearily.  “They already have.”
“So after all they did to you, you’re just going to let them walk away?” Lepidon responded.  “Without even an apology?”
“How are they supposed to apologize?” Elliot objected.  “Ruth was the one who finished Exam III in 10 minutes!  Ruth was the one who failed me with whatever diffusion equations for voltammetry is!”
“So it’s fine when she does it?” Lepidon asked.  “What about when you corrected Ruth?  Could she do any of that then?”
“No, she didn’t—” Elliot started.  “She got to…”  he trailed off.
“Then how did she do any of this now?” Lepidon asked.
“I don’t know!” Elliot snapped.  “How nice for her, she just—” He cut himself off quickly.  “No, I can’t be angry at Ruth!  Not again.”
“Why not?” Lepidon pressed.  “You have the full power of your anger, and you throw it away for Ruth?”
“What power?” Elliot snapped back.  “All it’s ever done is hurt me for Ruth!”
“Because you kept wasting it,” Lepidon replied.  “It’s easily enough power to stop all of them, even Ruth.  If you wanted, you could even map her pattern quantitatively.”
“Really? How?” Elliot demanded.
“I could show you this power,” Lepidon replied. “But only if you agree, of course.”
Elliot straightened.  “Fine.  Show me.”
Lepidon paused.  “And… what should I call you?”
Elliot replied firmly.  “Registrar.”
A dark purple mist surrounded Elliot, and when it receded, a formal suit and tie had materialized around his body, his handout had turned into a badge on his chest, and HUD glasses had formed on his face.

Lepidon spoke into Registrar’s head.  “Since Ruth is right here, we can investigate her directly.  Are you familiar with partial differential equations?”
“Um… no, not really?” Registrar responded.
“Not a problem.  Just approach her, and repeat exactly as I say.”
“Approach her?” Registrar objected.  “How do I know she won’t flinch it away?”
“She will,” Lepidon responded.  “But if you do as I tell you, that won’t hinder us.  Do you want to stop Ruth or not?”
“Fine,” Registrar exhaled.  He stood up and reluctantly walked down the steps toward the group.
At the sound of Registrar’s footsteps, Ruth immediately looked between him and the front of the class.  As her trembling returned, one hand flew to her mouth, and a soft squeak escaped.  Mira placed her hand on Ruth’s free hand, and several nearby students looked from Ruth to Registrar.
Victor stood up.  “Back off, Elliot,” he demanded.  “Haven’t you done enough?”
“I’m actually—” Registrar started.
What do you want?” Lucy asked suspiciously.
Lepidon spoke into Registrar’s head.  “Now.  Repeat after me.”
Registrar straightened.  “Actually, I was hoping I could learn something from Ruth.”
Ruth froze.  After a brief pause, Mira glanced at Ruth.  “Is that OK?”  Ruth blinked and nodded faintly.  Mira turned to Registrar.  “Make it quick.”

“So about diffusion equations and voltammetry—” Registrar started.
“You mean the thing from Ruth’s test?” Eric interrupted.
Registrar rolled his eyes.  “Yes, that,”  He exhaled.  “So I was wondering, for an irreversible redox couple, can you explain the current-time profile?  Like for a potential step experiment with a planar electrode?”
Ruth’s hand jolted towards the pile of pens behind her notebook.  Paige leaned in.  “Do you want to show us?” she asked gently.  Ruth nodded faintly.  Paige placed a pen from the pile in Ruth’s hand, and Mira slowly lifted her hand from Ruth’s.
Ruth loosely gripped the pen, and wrote in tiny font below the multicolored LSV diffusion setup she had already written in her notebook:
$i(t) = \dfrac{nFAD_O^{1/2} C_O^*}{\pi^{1/2}t^{1/2}}$
“I…” Ruth trailed off.
“Please, Ruth, it’s OK,” Victor said warmly to Ruth.  “He just asked for your help.”
“You just need to solve the partial differential equation,” Ruth said quietly.  “I-it’s not that hard.”
Claire gave Ruth an encouraging nod.  Registrar leaned over to look at the notebook.  “Hmm, I see.  Actually, I realized there was something else I didn’t get.”  He looked around.  “Sorry, does anyone have a pen and paper I can use?”
Mira, Paige and Max quickly shielded the pens on Ruth’s desk.  A student a few rows back held up a pen and a torn-out sheet of notebook paper.  Registrar nodded and accepted the paper. At Lepidon’s dictation, he wrote:
Given
$\dfrac{\partial C_O}{\partial t} = D_O \dfrac{\partial^2 C_O}{\partial x^2}$
with
$C_O(x,0) = C_O^*$
$\displaystyle\lim_{x\rightarrow \infty} C_O(x,t) = C_O^*$
$C_O(0,t) = 0$,
find $i(t) \equiv nFAD_O\left.\dfrac{\partial C_O}{\partial x}\right|_{x=0}$.

He returned the pen and held the piece of paper in front of Ruth.  Ruth flinched and didn’t turn back.
Sanjana took the piece of paper and blinked.  “Um, Ruth, is this OK?” she asked hesitantly.  Ruth nodded faintly.  Sanjana slipped it in front of Ruth.  “Here, Ruth,” she said gently.  
Ruth blinked at the equation in front of her and hunched over her table, covering the equation with her body.  As her whole body started quivering rapidly, she picked up the red pen on her table, and drew some hesitant strokes on the page.  Ruth turned to Registrar and handed him the piece of paper face-down.  “H-here, you j-j-just h-have to s-s-solve it-t…” she stammered shakily.

“What?” Lucy asked.  “She got it, didn’t she?”
Registrar blinked.  Lepidon spoke into his head.  “This is no longer simple.  Step outside.”
“But if I do that, they’ll…” Registrar’s voice trailed off.
“Let them,” Lepidon responded.  “Just say you understood and step outside.”
 Registrar exhaled.  “Um, thanks Ruth, this actually helped.”  Ruth went still again.
