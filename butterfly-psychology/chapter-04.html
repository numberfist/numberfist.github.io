“For this class?  Yes.  Outside of this class?  I don’t think so at least.”

The classroom was empty, except for Kimberley, Raghav and Nishant clustered around Professor Aubert, who stood behind the podium with an irritated expression on his face.

“…So cohomology can be given the structure of a graded algebra, and then we can give it a ring structure, right?”

“Then in that case, why can’t we do the same for homology, and make it a graded module over cohomology?”  She paused dramatically.  “All it takes is inverting the degrees of the elements of the cohomology ring, and you get the graded module structure.”

“Hold on,” Raghav said to Nishant.  “Is that actually a serious question?  Actually?”

Nishant narrowed his eyes at them into a quiet, icy glare, as the two walked out of the classroom.

“HA, so that’s your MAXIMUM score?” Luciano jeered.
“No, I said at least 60 percent, not at most,” Nishant retorted.  “And either way, the exam would be timed, so your little thought experiment doesn’t apply.”
Luciano waved his hand dismissively.  “Anyways, I’ll just leave that for you to think about yourself.”  He paused to lean back in a more relaxed way.  “Look at me, for example.  I go to the TA office hours but I just sit in a corner and do my homework by myself.  I don’t ask questions because I don’t need to.  And yet, I always see you and a few others asking questions to the TAs, and what do they do?  They tell you the answers to the homework.  So it’s easy for me to conclude that you are in fact too stupid to do the homework yourself, but after all that’s none of my business.  When you go take advanced classes later on, you’ll realize that you don’t know a thing and then you can see for yourself what you are.”
Nishant paused, then spoke evenly.  “So, care to explain why you have to tell me all this?”
“He’s just trying to help,” Harry interrupted.
“Yeah, exactly,” Sudhi repeated.  Jared nodded, smirking.
Luciano looked more closely at Nishant. “Look, I am not trying to be mean here.  I just want you to see the truth as I see it, since you can’t see it yourself.  I know it’s difficult but it has to be told.  If you can handle honestly, I can give you honest advice.”  He shrugged his shoulders.  “After all, in the end, how people perceive your intelligence should not matter at all.  Look at me, I don’t care about how people see my intelligence.  So don’t worry about it, especially since you have no way to make that perception of you better.  What you can instead do is improve your PERSONALITY instead.”
